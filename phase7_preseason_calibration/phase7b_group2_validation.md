## Structural finding — the "source disagreement" cases are largely a scale artifact

The mandate asks whether one rating is *methodologically incomparable*. Measured
across all 138 workbook teams (mean-centered exactly as the workbook does):

| Source | SD | Min | Max | Range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SP+ | **13.20** | −31.91 | +32.29 | 64.20 |
| FPI | **11.33** | −19.30 | +28.70 | 48.00 |
| TeamRankings | 13.79 | −28.50 | +32.30 | 60.80 |

**SP+ is a systematically wider scale than FPI** (SD ratio 1.165; best-fit
slope SP+ ≈ 1.117 × FPI). Consequently the mean |SP+ − FPI| gap grows
monotonically down the board: **1.76 (top tier) → 2.24 (middle) → 4.53 (bottom
tier).** The workbook **mean-centers but does not standardize**, so at the
bottom of FBS a large point-gap is expected *even when the sources agree about
the team*.

Rank-space (scale-neutral) settles it:

| Team | SP+ rank | FPI rank | TR rank | Rank gap | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Charlotte** | 138 | 126 | 136 | **12** | All three agree Charlotte is near the FBS floor → the 17.8-pt "disagreement" is **scale, not football**. Resolved. |
| **Southern Miss** | 131 | 87 | 122 | **44** | A genuine disagreement survives the scale correction → real uncertainty. |

This is an observation about interpretation only. **No recommendation to change
weights, normalization, or any workbook logic is made or implied.**
