2026 VSiN COLLEGE FOOTBALL BETTING GUIDE
As much as college football has changed, some things still remain the same. Group of Five programs continue to provide a feeder 
system for the deeper-pocketed Power Four schools in terms of coaching staffs. The G5 - now G6 with the return of the Pac-12 - 
presently doubles as a minor league system of sorts in the NIL era, where players are hoping to get called up to a major conference and 
a significant payday. There used to be a big imbalance, where mostly coaches would preach brotherhood, family, and loyalty and then 
bounce to collect a bigger bag. Players have that opportunity now, too.
There are 138 FBS schools this season, and 35 of them (25.4%) have new head coaches, if we include guys who are interims or had 
the interim tag removed. While not quite the 31.3% in the NFL with 10 new sideline bosses for 32 teams, it is another year of drastic 
turnover with coaches and players in college football. Hitting the playbooks is becoming more difficult than hitting the textbooks, as 
some of these players will go through multiple coordinators and head coaches while they’re in school.
With 30 and 28 head coaching changes over the last two seasons, respectively, we’ve seen some teams gel very quickly and others 
struggle to have everything come together. These are not professionals; they are student-athletes. While most of them are paid to play, 
maturity levels vary wildly at this age and some schemes require more focus and learning than others. It is important to know the 
teams with the new head coaches for a variety of reasons, so let’s look at them and try to pinpoint some teams that could improve right 
away, improve gradually, or could be in for a rough year.
by Adam Burke
