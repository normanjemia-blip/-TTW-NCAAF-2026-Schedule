Will the Cougars’ new coach bring Moore or less?
As offensive coordinator at Missouri the past three years, Kirby 
Moore designed dynamic offenses around dual-threat quarterbacks. 
He is Washington State’s third coach in three years, following Jake 
Dickert (2024) and Jimmy Rogers (2025). Dickert moved on to Wake 
Forest, and Rogers took the Iowa State job while also taking several 
players with him. The Cougars went a combined 15-11 the past two 
years and still have talent in the program, but Moore had to hit the 
transfer portal to replenish the roster and found a promising dual-
threat quarterback to help the transition. It’s doubtful Washington 
State will achieve a third straight winning season while rebuilding, 
so expect less from Moore’s first season.
Is the sky the limit for Caden Pinnick?
At UC Davis, Pinnick was voted Big Sky Freshman of the Year after 
passing for 3,206 yards and 32 touchdowns while also rushing 
for 437 yards. The 6-footer completed 70% of his passes and his 
running ability excites Moore, who outrecruited Colorado to get 
the quarterback he coveted. Pinnick led UC Davis to nine wins 
and a spot in the FCS playoffs. The Cougars’ top receiver, senior 
speedster Tony Freeman, returns along with leading rusher Kirby 
Vorhees. Freeman had 54 receptions and Vorhees, who transferred 
from South Dakota State prior to last season, had 576 yards and five 
touchdowns. The offense brings back a few experienced players, 
including senior tackle Ashton Tripp, and welcomes Florida transfer 
receiver Tank Hawkins. The offense will be the obvious strength of 
the team with Moore designing the plays and Pinnick, Vorhees and 
Freeman doing a majority of the playmaking.
Will a defense with no returning starters be a disaster?
Blowout losses to North Texas and Washington aside, Washington 
State was mostly outstanding on defense a year ago while allowing 
20.2 PPG to rank 27th in the nation. New coordinator Trent Bray, 
the former head coach at Oregon State, will need to work miracles 
to make the Cougars competitive on the defensive side. Senior 
end Linus Zunk transferred from Vanderbilt and senior corner 
Jalil Tucker transferred from Oregon as Bray combed the portal to 
find pieces to the puzzle. Senior linebacker Keith Brown, a former 
Oregon transfer who missed last season with an arm injury, will be 
another key player. Bray was a good hire, but this defense has a long 
way to go, and the Cougars could get into many high-scoring games.
