2026 VSiN COLLEGE FOOTBALL BETTING GUIDE
The college football landscape is changing 
literally and figuratively this season, as 
the Pac-12 is back with eight teams, the 
Mountain West looks a lot different, and a 
team from California will play in the MAC. 
Home-field advantage is probably more 
dynamic than ever, in the sense that it 
depends on a variety of factors. Having one 
simple baseline probably isn’t enough, but 
having something is better than nothing 
and that has been my approach over the last 
several seasons.
Playing at home is never really going to 
be a bad thing, so there is still an inherent 
“advantage”, even though its value will 
always be subjective. Weather, rest, travel, 
and all of the usual factors come into play, 
but there are others. A night game in Death 
Valley (either one) is going to carry more 
weight than a noon kickoff against a Group 
of Six school. A school where the basketball 
program outpaces the football program could see home-field lessened once 
November rolls around and students are more invested in hoops. How invested 
are the alums and adults in that particular program nowadays? It’s very hard to 
pin down these qualitative factors.
But, at least if we have some kind of value in mind, we can adjust accordingly, 
whether we actually attempt to quantify all of those variables or just keep them 
in mind when handicapping the game. To find that baseline, I’ve decided to take 
a three-year sample of home records, as that would seem to capture the team’s 
current state and also create a sample size large enough to minimize some noise. 
With over 100 different head coaches and thousands of transfers in FBS over 
the last three seasons, even three years may be tricky, but it seems like a happy 
medium.
Based on the straight up (SU) and against the spread (ATS) home win percentages 
for these teams, I’ve created a series of buckets. The standard home-field 
advantage I’ve settled on is two points. For teams that sit between 40% and 
59.99% at home, their home-field advantage is two points. For teams above or 
below those thresholds, the HFA varies.
For this season, there are 24 teams with my max HFA of 3.5 points for having a 
SU + ATS Win% of 70% or higher. There are 16 teams with 3 points of HFA for 
a SU + ATS Win% of 65-69.99%. There are 24 teams with 2.5 points of HFA for 
60-64.99%.
On the flip side, there are eight teams with 1.5 points of HFA for 35.01-39.99% 
and eight teams with 1 point of HFA for 35% or lower. Again, I want to stress 
that I consider these numbers to be starting points for creating point spreads on 
games. All of those factors and variables mentioned above should be considered 
on a case-by-case basis.
The home records are courtesy of Steve Makinen and his extensive 
database, so my thanks go out to our VSiN Analytics Guru.
Here are those teams for the 2026 season.
With the HFA numbers established for all 138 FBS teams, those are then put 
together with my college football power ratings to produce a game spread. If 
my power ratings have Ohio State 14 points better than USC and the game is in 
Columbus, that would make my line on the game Buckeyes -17.5 with their 3.5 
points of home-field advantage.
This season, I expect to have some deviations on a week-to-week basis in 
conference play, since we have Sacramento State in the MAC, Northern Illinois 
in the Mountain West, and Texas State in the Pac-12, just as some examples 
of conference realignment and how those teams will be visiting new venues, 
playing at elevation, or having long travel with some odd kickoff times. Similarly, 
teams that go to them will be doing something different.
As I continue to say, home-field advantage is an “inexact science”, but a true case 
of ‘something is better than nothing’ and at least there is some sort of criteria that 
teams meet in order to have the values that they do.
