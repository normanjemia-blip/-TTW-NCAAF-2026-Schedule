Are there any weaknesses on offense?
Carr had a 24/6 TD/INT ratio last season and added three more scores 
on the ground, though he was hardly the rushing threat that Riley 
Leonard was. The depth behind Carr is a major worry if something 
were to happen to him, but he spent most of the spring and summer 
as the favorite to win the Heisman Trophy. The schedule surely has 
a lot to do with that, as anything under 11 wins would be a bit of a 
surprise, so he should be putting up numbers and winning games 
for a very visible program. While Jeremiyah Love and Jadarian Price 
are gone, taking over 2,000 rushing yards and 29 rushing scores with 
them, Aneyas Williams should be able to keep the bar high at that 
position. Similarly, while Jordan Faison is the only notable returnee 
at wide receiver, Jaden Greathouse is back after missing nearly all 
of last season due to injury. Plus, the offensive line looks very good, 
including the return of Charles Jagusah. So, in a word, no.
Are there any weaknesses on defense?
The TL;DR version of this question is one simple word - no. Unlike 
a lot of premier programs with heavy personnel losses, second-
year DC Chris Ash has his top six tacklers back. The Irish were one 
of 11 teams to hold opponents to fewer than three yards per carry 
and only eight teams allowed fewer yards on a per-play basis. Notre 
Dame has had a lot of coaches and players roll through in the past 
seven years and they’ve only allowed more than 19.7 PPG one time. 
Hell, they’ve allowed under five yards per play in each of the last 
three seasons under Freeman. They are +31 in TO margin over 
the last two seasons, which is one area of concern, but not if Carr 
takes care of the ball. The defense will go ball-hawking again after 
securing 21 interceptions last season.
Anything else?
It feels like I’ve said about all I can say about this team. Notre 
Dame’s two close losses last season were followed up by a bunch 
of blowout wins, as they outgained their other 10 opponents by 
more than 1,800 yards in total and outscored them by nearly 300 
points. As long as the Irish avoid cluster injuries, the goal of a 
perfect season is very attainable, and that includes the CFP as well. 
It should also be noted that Notre Dame only plays four true road 
games - Purdue, North Carolina, BYU, and Syracuse. Two of their 
“road” games are in neutral settings, with one in Green Bay and one 
in Foxboro. The Irish get an extra week to prepare for Navy’s triple-
option with a well-timed bye that comes the week after they play in 
elevation at BYU.
