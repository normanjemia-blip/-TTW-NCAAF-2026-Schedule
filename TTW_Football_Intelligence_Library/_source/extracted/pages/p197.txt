Will Chadwell fix the passing game?
It’s hardly a coincidence that Chadwell was getting ready to 
leave Coastal Carolina just as Grayson McCall was running out of 
eligibility. Chadwell did get former Chanticleer Ethan Vasko to 
transfer in, but that didn’t really work out well, as he posted a 10/12 
TD/INT ratio, threw for under 2,000 yards, and had just 2.6 yards per 
carry on 100 attempts. Vasko is still on the roster, but Chadwell also 
went to the transfer portal store and picked out Deshawn Purdie, 
formerly at Wake Forest. He also picked up some WR transfers from 
Charleston Southern and Indiana. Chadwell’s offense is predicated 
on a lot of pre-snap motion to get guys into space, but Vasko didn’t 
make those throws. Purdie is only a 50% passer over 282 attempts. 
While Chadwell has options, it remains to be seen if any of them are 
good.
But maybe he doesn’t have to?
One of the top free agent pickups in the conference is former 
Army running back Kanye Udoh. After running for 1,117 yards in 
2024, Udoh was able to transfer out of West Point and wound up at 
Arizona State, where he didn’t see much action. Now he’s back on 
the East Coast to fill the big cleats of Evan Dickens, who was a bright 
spot for an otherwise lackluster offense with 1,339 yards and 16 TD 
out of the backfield. Peyton Jones from Duke and Kam Davis from 
Florida State seem promising as well. When Chadwell had McCall, 
it was a pass-first offense. Now that he doesn’t, it’s a run-first offense 
with 508 carries against 316 pass attempts last season. All the QBs 
need to do is complete enough passes to keep defenses honest.
Is new DC Shawn Quinn the right man for the job?
Coordinator hires can be a huge deal at any program, but especially 
in the G6 conferences because the talent levels vary so much. 
Quinn, who was the outside linebackers coach at Virginia Tech, 
went 16-6 as the head coach at Savannah State before joining the 
Hokies. Ironically, Quinn was the DC the year before Chadwell took 
over at Charleston Southern, so the two have probably crossed 
paths, but never worked together until now. Leading tackler Derrell 
Farrar is back, but this is mostly a blank slate for Quinn. That’s good 
because what hurt Liberty last season was allowing 0.7 more yards 
per carry than the two previous seasons and a linebackers coach 
turned DC should be able to fix that.
