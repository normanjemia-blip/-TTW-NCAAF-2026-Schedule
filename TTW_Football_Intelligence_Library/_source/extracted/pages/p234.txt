Aside from an uncharacteristic 3-9 
showing in 2021, the Bobcats have 
been one of the most consistent 
G5 teams in the nation. Last year’s 
9-4 mark was the first time since 
that ‘21 season that Ohio didn’t win 
10+ games, but given everything 
that they went through, it was 
another really good season. It 
wasn’t surprising that the transfer 
of power from Frank Solich to 
Tim Albin resulted in a one-year 
blip, but now the Bobcats have 
another new head coach in John 
Hauser. While they’ve kept all of 
their promotions internal, from 
Albin to Brian Smith to Hauser, 
who was the interim head coach 
for the Frisco Bowl win over UNLV, 
you have to wonder if there’s a big 
impact on one of these seasons.
Perhaps this could be the one, as 
Hauser, who was the defensive 
coordinator, is now looking at 
a program with one returning 
starter on offense, the loss of a 
multi-year QB in Parker Navarro, 
the loss of near-1,400-yard rusher 
Sieh Bangura, who transferred 
back into the program prior to last 
season, and a noticeably lackluster 
transfer portal class.

It is entirely possible that an 
established, steady program can 
withstand and overcome all of 
the doom and gloom addressed 
above, but I have my doubts. Given 
how the Bobcats have had tenured 
starting QBs like Tettleton and the 
Rourkes, I won’t be the least bit 
shocked if they take some lumps 
and embrace a transitional year 
with Davis, the three-star QB from 
the Columbus area, to set him up to 
follow the long line of productive 
predecessors. While the Bobcats 
do avoid reigning champ Western 
Michigan, they get Miami and 
Toledo over a three-week stretch, 
have to go out to Sacramento, and 
have some non-conference toss-
ups. My projection is 6.91 wins, but 
I could see a down year.
