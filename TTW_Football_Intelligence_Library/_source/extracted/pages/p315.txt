What does the post-Diego Pavia era look like?
It’s always hard to replace a starting quarterback. It’s even harder 
when that quarterback went 17-9 as a starter, led the program to 
its first ever 10-win season and finished second in the Heisman 
Trophy voting his final season. But that is exactly what Lea and 
the Commodore program have to do. Lea told the media that 
Vanderbilt will have a competition for the starting job, but it seems 
that five-star freshman Jared Curtis is the likely starter when the 
Commodores face Austin Peay to open the season. Curtis was 
the second-best prospect of his class, according to 247. Early 
projections have him as a potential first-round pick. He is not 
exactly a dual-threat, but with a past playing history as a running 
back, Curtis can extend plays and be potentially dangerous as a 
designed runner. Still, he is a true freshman taking over a program 
in the SEC. Equally talented signal-callers have struggled with less 
demanding circumstances in college football.
Can the offense continue to punish opponents on the ground?
Vanderbilt was a very good rush offense in 2025. The Commodores 
averaged 176 yards per game and 5.6 yards per carry. Much of that 
was due to the presence of Pavia, who led the team with 862 yards 
rushing. But Sedrick Alexander and Makhilyn Young — the next 
two leading rushers — are back in the fold, giving Lea something 
to work with. Alexander averaged 5.5 yards per carry and showed 
some physicality as a runner by averaging 3.43 yards per carry 
after contact. Young was explosive. He ran for 414 yards on only 47 
carries. He averaged 4.21 yards per carry after contact himself. The 
talent is there, but is the offensive line? Four starters are gone, and 
they added just one FBS transfer to the position in the portal. This, 
coupled with Pavia’s absence, likely means a stark drop-off on the 
ground.
Will the defense do enough to make up for the potentially 
declining offense?
Vanderbilt was an underrated group on defense in 2025. The 
Commodores limited opponents to just 3.7 yards per carry and 22.8 
points per game. They finished 23rd in opponent EPA per rush and 
49th in success rate on the ground. However, the secondary was a 
weakness. It ranked 129th in EPA per dropback and 111th in success 
rate. Seven starters are back, and most reside in the front seven. All 
eyes will be on the secondary this season. Lea added some bodies in 
the portal — including former four-star prospect Ricardo Jones from 
Clemson — but there will be quite a bit of internal promotion on the 
backend. Can the new faces help improve the defense’s weakness? 
It will be a necessity considering the obvious growing pains on the 
horizon for the offense.
