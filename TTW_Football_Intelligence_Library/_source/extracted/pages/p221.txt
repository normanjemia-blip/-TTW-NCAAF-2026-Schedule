Are the Falcons set up for success?
Bowling Green had injuries, as every team does, but it seemed like 
last season was a series of fact-finding missions for George and his 
staff to isolate the players that best fit the system. Austyn Dendy 
looks like the feature back and had a solid freshman season with 4.6 
yards per carry, but his 108 carries barely outpaced Chris McMillian 
with 84 and Cameron Pettaway with 72. Kaderris Roberts also had 
48 carries. George was trying out everybody to see who would stick, 
and it was ultimately Dendy who did. This season, I would expect 
a tighter rotation with Dendy and Charleston Southern transfer 
Ke’Marion Baldwin, who had nearly five yards per carry for the 
Buccaneers. Similarly, a lot of guys that played, but maybe weren’t 
focal points, now get chances at WR and on the offensive line, 
including the tight ends with a lot of 12 personnel in this scheme.
Will the defense take a step forward?
The offense only mustered 20.5 PPG and just 4.9 YPP, so the defense 
was under a lot of pressure. Linebackers coach Joe Bowden, who 
played with George as a member of the Tennessee Titans, is now 
the DC of a group that allowed just 24.3 PPG, but 5.6 YPP. Gideon 
Lampron basically never left the field and he’ll be missed with 
119 tackles, including 15 tackles for loss, but the defense was a lot 
like the offense in that a lot of guys played. Some of the expected 
starters were with George at Tennessee State two seasons ago, but 
either didn’t start at BG or had transferred elsewhere. Even if the 
defense doesn’t take a step forward, a similar baseline coupled with 
a better offense would be effective.
Did Eddie George learn some important lessons?
The Falcons had those two very impressive wins over Liberty and 
Toledo, but also blew leads against Kent State, Akron, and Eastern 
Michigan that would have made the season look a lot different and 
may have even led to bowl eligibility. The no-show against Central 
Michigan the week after the Toledo game was also a good teaching 
moment for everybody. Keep in mind, when George took over at 
Tennessee State, he hadn’t really coached anywhere else. Instead, 
he had been an adjunct professor at Ohio State and Vanderbilt and 
had been doing some acting. Even though he’s been around football 
all his life, coaching, especially in the current CFB landscape, is 
a lot different and his first recruiting cycle at the FBS level seems 
promising.
