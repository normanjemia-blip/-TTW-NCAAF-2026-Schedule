Can Cook keep the offense cooking?
Kody Cook is the new OC after serving as the wide receivers coach 
last season. He’s also the new QB coach with some shifting across 
the staff. The Monarchs relied very heavily on their top three 
receivers last season, as only three guys had 11+ catches and they 
had 762, 719, and 667 yards while nobody else had more than 200. 
All three of those guys are gone, so Cook’s old position group needs 
to be reimagined, but this should be a run-heavy team again. Devin 
Roche had 5.7 yards per carry, not nearly as good as Trequan Jones’ 
7.5 yards per pop, plus JUCO transfer Trey Hall was originally an Ole 
Miss commit. Quinn Henicle and Jaxon Potter will have skill guys 
to work with and Cook, who played at Kansas State and coached at 
Tennessee, was a logical guy to promote with Decker’s departure.
Who gets more reps as the starting QB?
Henicle is the holdover and the guy who knows this system and 
scheme, but he also wasn’t very good last season. He ran for 4.8 
yards per carry on 44 attempts and only completed 42.5% of his 
passes, as he was just 11-for-25 against a skeleton crew USF team 
in the bowl game. Meanwhile, Potter, who was a higher-graded 
recruit, was willing to come all the way from Washington State and 
head to the other coast as a California native. That said, Henicle 
was recruited to some FCS schools with good rushing attacks and 
also Army, so he was clearly viewed as an impact QB in the ground 
game. Neither guy looks poised to top Joseph’s numbers, but Rahne 
needs to get this right and fix it early in the season, if he doesn’t 
coming out of camp.
Should we buy or sell this defense?
Rahne, an offensive-minded guy, seems to have finally rounded that 
side of the ball into form, going up in yards per carry and points 
per game in each of the last three seasons. However, the biggest 
separator for the Monarchs last season was Blake Seiler’s defense. 
That group shaved off 9.5 PPG and 0.9 YPP from the previous 
season, holding opponents to just 18.5 PPG and 4.8 YPP. As a team, 
ODU outgained opponents by 1.9 yards per play. Seiler saw the 
sack total go from 19 to 26 to 39 in a three-year span and put forth 
a top-25 third-down defense. Seiler also sees the return of two 
linebackers who started in 2024, but missed most of last season, 
and has returning starters at each level of the defense. I’ll buy that 
they’re better than 2024, but worse than 2025.
