What’s going on at the quarterback position? 
Devon Dampier is back for the Utes after throwing for 2,490 yards 
with 24 touchdowns and only five interceptions last year. He 
also rushed for 835 yards and 10 scores. Dampier is a dynamic 
dual-threat option under center, and he showed some minor 
improvements in accuracy during his first season with Utah. 
However, this team is going to give Byrd Ficklin snaps to see what 
he can do. At times, this approach will be tough for opponents to 
stop, but what happens when Utah faces elite defenses? As the 
saying goes, “when you have two quarterbacks, you really have 
none.” Will the Utes be able to consistently convert in key third-and-
long situations? If not, their schedule could get the better of them. 
Will the offensive line be this team’s Achilles heel? 
Utah normally has big, burly dudes along the offensive line, and 
blocking is rarely an issue with this program. However, the Utes 
don’t return a single starter from last year’s team, and the only 
projected starter who was consistently starting games in 2025 is 
Montana State transfer Cedric Jefferson. Utah does have some 
impressive young pieces to play up front this year, including true 
freshman Kelvin Obot. He looks the part of a future pro. But there’s 
nothing that suggests this will be one of the better units in the Big 
12, and this offense won’t look great without strong blocking. 
Is this defense good enough for the Utes to compete at the highest 
level? 
It’s brutal that Scalley was this team’s defensive coordinator last 
year, yet the team wasn’t able to keep Snowden and Henry Daley 
around. The Utes now enter the season with some major concerns 
on defense, which is quite new for a program that is consistently 
excellent on this side of the ball. Utah only has four returning 
starters in the projected lineup, and it’s the defensive line and 
linebackers that look shaky. The Utes should be extremely tough to 
throw against, but will they be able to stop the run? That’s looking 
like a potential weakness, and being unable to stop opponents from 
picking up chunk yardage has a trickle-down effect that impacts the 
secondary.
