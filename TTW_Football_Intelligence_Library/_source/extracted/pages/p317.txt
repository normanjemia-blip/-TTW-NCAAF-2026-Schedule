2026 VSiN COLLEGE FOOTBALL BETTING GUIDE
The Sun Belt remains the only conference in college football to still use divisions, as the 14 teams in the conference are 
split right down the middle to form the East and West. Traditionally, the East has been stronger with teams like App 
State and Coastal Carolina and only got stronger when James Madison and Marshall joined the conference. The West is 
headlined primarily by Louisiana and Troy, but Louisiana Tech is a conference newcomer this season looking to make 
some noise. Texas State jumped ship to join the reimagined Pac-12.
Conference favorite James Madison has experienced big changes once again, as Bob Chesney followed predecessor Curt 
Cignetti to the Big Ten. Billy Napier, lovingly (sort of) referred to as “Sun Belt Billy” for his time at Louisiana, is back in 
the conference now after falling short of expectations at Florida. Incredibly, he is one of just three new head coaches 
in the conference, as we usually see a lot of turnover in the Sun Belt and similar leagues. The others are Ryan Beard at 
Coastal Carolina and Blake Anderson, another former Sun Belt boss, at Southern Miss.
This is a conference that is important to know because of the early-season weeknight games across the ESPN family of 
networks, so you’re going to get a few standalone Sun Belt nights where those teams are front and center before we really 
get into the NBA and NHL seasons. There can be some excellent betting opportunities for those who are keeping tabs on 
the conference.
Of course, the carrot of the College Football Playoff berth that dangles in front of the highest-ranked G6 conference 
champ is something that adds intrigue to the season. Last year, it was James Madison who locked that up and put forth 
a decent effort against Oregon, covering the 21-point number in a 17-point loss. The Dukes are +270 to run it back in the 
“Fun Belt”, but they are +1500 to make the CFP for a second straight year. Old Dominion is the second shot at +460 before 
we get to Louisiana (7-1) and Troy (8-1), so the East looks to be outpacing the West yet again.
