Will the string run out at the quarterback position?
Whether you want to credit the scouting and talent evaluation, the 
system, the stability of the program, or just outright good luck, Ohio 
has been on a generational run of QBs since Tyler Tettleton showed 
up in 2009. Ohio’s three all-time leading passers have all come 
within the last 20 years with Tettleton, Nathan Rourke, and Kurtis 
Rourke. While Navarro had trouble taking care of the ball with just 
a 27/22 TD/INT ratio in his two seasons as a starter, he ran for nearly 
2,000 yards with 27 rushing scores. Hauser and OC Scott Isphording 
have options, including returnee Nick Poulos, who redshirted last 
season, New Hampshire transfer Matt Vezza, who ran for 600 yards, 
and incoming freshman Levi Davis. But, the bar is set so high here 
that virtually any of those options are likely to fall short.
Is QB the only concern on offense?
Hardly. The offensive line features all new starters. While pretty 
much everybody has been in the system for a long time, they are 
unproven, which is a major concern with Bangura’s 1,392 yards 
and 15 rushing TDs and the team’s top four wide receivers in 
yardage, including a 1,000-yard pass-catcher in Chase Hendricks 
all elsewhere. Even in the transfer portal era, Ohio has had a lot of 
continuity and returning production year over year, or have at least 
had high-upside players waiting in the wings. The offense looks 
unrecognizable this season. And, as potent as they’ve been on a per-
play basis, with 6.1 and 6.2 YPP over the last two seasons, they still 
haven’t scored 30 PPG since 2022 when they had a +12 TO margin.
Will the defense save the day?
It’s possible, but it is interesting to note that Hauser was the co-
DC at Miami of Ohio, so he was one of the few external hires in 
recent seasons. I’m not sure if that will matter or not. Kurt Mattix 
is back to be the DC after shacking up in Charlotte with Albin last 
season. The attrition on the defensive side is a lot lighter than the 
offensive side, but still noticeable. With the top two tacklers gone, 
top defensive back Tarik Pearson, and most of last season’s very 
low total of 22 sacks, backups will have to step up and transfers will 
have to acclimate quickly. After racking up 36, 36, and 35 sacks over 
the previous three seasons, the 22 sacks were the fewest since that 
ill-fated ‘21 season with 17.
