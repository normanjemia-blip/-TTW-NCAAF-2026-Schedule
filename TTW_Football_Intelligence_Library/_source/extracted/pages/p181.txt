Will Gary Patterson be able to put some fight into the USC 
defense?
Defense has seemed like a bit of an afterthought during Riley’s 
early tenure, but it did show the most improvement last season 
under D’Anton Lynn as the Trojans ranked No. 51 scoring defense 
(23 points per game), No. 47 pass defense (207.5 yards per game) 
and No. 58 run defense (143.2 yards per game). Lynn got the call 
to return to his alma mater Penn State as defensive coordinator, 
so Patterson returns to full-time coaching for the first time since 
2021. Patterson switches the defense to a 4-2-5 where there is a 
lot of depth up front including defensive ends Kameryn Crawford 
(5.5 sacks, 10.5 TFLs) and Braylan Shelby (4.5 sacks), defensive 
tackles Jahkeem Stewart and Jide Abasiri plus the addition of 
Michigan State transfer Alex VanSumeren (52 tackles) to improve 
a run defense that ranked 12th in the conference. The back seven 
has talent but must replace All-American Bishop Fitzgerald in the 
secondary.
Who takes over at receiver for Makai Lemon and Ja’Kobi Lane?
Between Lemon, the Biletnikoff Award winner, and Lane, 1,900 
receiving yards and 15 touchdowns are gone. Tanook Hines (34-
561-2) will get the first crack at being Maiava’s top target. NC State 
transfer Terrell Anderson had 628 yards last year on 16.1 yards per 
reception. The talent level of the group appears to be a little down 
from USC’s typically high level which means the Trojans may run 
the ball more with the tandem of Miller and Jordan, who combined 
for over 1,500 yards and 15 touchdowns, running behind an 
offensive line that returns all five starters. 
Can the Trojans win away from home and beat someone good?
In Riley’s four seasons at USC, the Trojans are 5-11 vs. ranked 
opposition. This season there are likely as many as five ranked 
opponents (Oregon, Washington, Penn State, Ohio State, and 
Indiana) on the schedule. The good news is that Oregon and Ohio 
State pay a visit to the LA Coliseum where the Trojans went 7-0 
last season. However, the Trojans are only 10-10 in true Big 10 
road games under Riley. They get a couple tests with trips to Penn 
State and Indiana. If USC wants to be taken seriously as a playoff 
contender again, then the Trojans must win more consistently on 
the road.
