Hype or hope?
Not all highly-touted recruits make it. The transfer portal gives 
players with that kind of pedigree the chance to go to a G6 program 
and attempt to get back on the radar. At USF, most of those players 
are quarterbacks, as Hartline and OC Tim Beck got Michael Van 
Buren by way of Mississippi State and LSU and Luke Kromenhoek 
by way of Florida State and Mississippi State. MVB did have some 
success at those two stops, including an 8/2 TD/INT ratio at LSU last 
season with four starts and some relief efforts. There are so many 
unproven players on this side of the ball and it’s fair to wonder if 
Hartline is a program-builder or was just able to thrive with the 
foundation already set at Ohio State.
What the Beck?
You can understand why Hartline turned to Tim Beck as the OC, as 
the 60-year-old has been coaching since the late ‘80s. It took him 35 
years to get his first head coaching gig and it didn’t go particularly 
well at Coastal Carolina with a 20-18 record. So, he’s an OC again 
and I find it underwhelming, personally. Most of the guys acquired 
via free agency didn’t play very much at their previous schools, but 
247Sports graded USF’s portal haul the best in the conference and 
58th nationally with 44 total commits. That’s a lot of scouting and a 
lot of new faces.
Could the defense outpace the offense in 2026?
While the hiring of Beck is pretty bland in my eyes, the hiring of 
Josh Aldridge as DC is not. Aldridge has been working at conference 
rival East Carolina under head coach Blake Harrell, who has proven 
to be an outstanding defensive mind and a pretty darn good HC. 
Stopping the run is the key to success in this conference. Most 
teams run the ball well or have run-heavy schemes. Aldridge’s ECU 
unit allowed just 3.6 yards per carry. Everybody seems to allow 
passing yards and 60+% completion percentages, but the teams that 
stop the run position themselves for success. That was true of USF 
last season with just 3.7 YPC. This may not be a polished defense, 
but it is a big front seven.
