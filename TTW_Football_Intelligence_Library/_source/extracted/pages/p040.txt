2026 VSiN COLLEGE FOOTBALL BETTING GUIDE
Last year was an eye-opener for my “world-famous” college football 
stability system. Prior to the 2025 campaign, I shared my concern about 
how program stability was changing, largely because of the transfer 
portal. However, since the system had only suffered a single losing 
season since 2013, I figured I owed it to VSiN readers to go with the 
methodology “as-is.” Well, unfortunately, that backfired a bit for me 
and for everyone who has benefited from the Stability System for many 
years, as the final record wound up being just 30-36 ATS. I promised that 
I would dig into the nuts and bolts of the concept to see whether what 
I was doing still made sense in this new era. I did and now have some 
new data to share. 
The College Football Stability System is for the first four weeks of every 
season (Weeks 0, 1, 2, and 3). It is the foremost strategy I employ every 
year to find value early. The thought behind it is that teams in more 
stable year-to-year situations are better bets early, while those that have 
undergone significant offseason change should be faded. I feel the 
logic is fundamentally sound. Returning fewer starters, starting over 
at quarterback, and welcoming new head coaches or coordinators are 
always hurdles for teams.
One of the biggest areas of transition for teams nowadays is the transfer 
of starting quarterbacks. To me, the position is of such importance that 
a transfer quarterback for a team, even if he has three years of starting 
experience somewhere else, I still consider him a NEW quarterback, or 
more effectively, a change at QB. He is still learning new schemes, and 
while his veteran experience may lessen some of the bumps along the 
way, there are still hurdles to overcome early on with this new player 
in this most key position. There can be degrees to this; however, in the 
current era, some QBs, such as Rocco Becht of Penn State, followed 
their former school’s head coach and OC to a new school. To me, this 
is still a big change. So, to clarify how I am dealing with the transfer 
portal starters, here is your answer: I consider former starters acquired 
through the transfer portal at non-QB positions as starters.
This is where the new numbers and changes will come in. I have 
conducted regression analysis on my College Football Stability System 
and here are the results. First off, my results still did show that the 
FIRST FOUR WEEKS (0, 1, 2, and 3) were the most impacted by stability. 
So, I will be sticking with that. Also, since most of the changes I’ve seen 
in the college football world have occurred since the COVID season, I 
have looked back at the results of the various stability factors in the first 
four weeks after 2021. Here are those results. Note that these trends only 
include FBS vs. FBS games.
•	
NEW head coaches are 91-153 SU and 110-132 ATS (45.4%) in the 
first four weeks of the CFB season versus returning head coaches 
since 2021 
•	
NEW offensive coordinators are 157-203 SU and 177-178 ATS 
(49.9%) in the first four weeks of the CFB season versus returning 
offensive coordinators since 2021 
•	
NEW defensive coordinators are 165-198 SU and 163-195 ATS 
(45.5%) in the first four weeks of the CFB season versus returning 
defensive coordinators since 2021 
•	
NEW starting quarterbacks are 159-178 SU and 157-172 ATS (47.7%) 
in the first four weeks of the CFB season versus returning defensive 
coordinators since 2021 
•	
Teams with 0-7 total returning starters are 21-60 SU and 40-41 ATS 
(49.4%) in the first four weeks of the CFB season versus teams 
returning 8+ since 2021 
•	
Teams with 17+ total returning starters are 107-89 SU and 108-86 
ATS (55.7%) in the first four weeks of the CFB season versus teams 
returning 16 or fewer since 2021
From this info, bettors can certainly discern that having new head 
coaches, new defensive coordinators, and many returning starters are 
the most impactful offseason developments.
I also recognized that CONFERENCE GAMES on my stability system 
were pulling down the winning percentages. In fact, teams with an old 
stability edge of seven points or more in conference games were only 
winning around 49.5% ATS. 
The other two things I found that were negatively impacting my Stability 
System results were 1) games with point spreads of 30+ and 2) leaving 
out teams with an edge of 6 on the stability scores.
With the stats above in mind, my official new College Football Stability 
System(s) will read as follows:
Play ON any team with a STABILITY SCORE EDGE of 6+ in non-conference 
games in the first four weeks of the season (weeks 0, 1, 2, 3), assuming the 
game DOES NOT have a point spread of -30 or higher for either team. (335-
222 SU and 302-243-12 ATS, 55.4% since 2013)
AND 
Play AGAINST any team with STABILITY SCORES of 0-6 in non-conference 
games in the first four weeks of the season (weeks 0, 1, 2, 3) versus teams 
with higher scores, assuming the game DOES NOT have a point spread of 
-30 or higher for either team (FADE record 259-136 SU and 217-173-4 ATS, 
55.6% since 2013)
Doing regression on the tweaked Stability Score Edge System, I found 
that over the last 13 years, there were only two losing seasons, one of 
them being last year. The second angle, teams with 0-6 scores, even lost 
a year ago, as they were 22-26 ATS. In my opinion, both systems are still 
very viable.

will consider a play for this thought process in non-conference games.

first two playable games for Week 0.
