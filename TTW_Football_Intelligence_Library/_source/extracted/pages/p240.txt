For a G6 team to return the 
conference’s Offensive Player of 
the Year with the current state 
of college football seems rare. 
For that player to be the starting 
QB is even rarer. Then, add in a 
1,000-yard running back from 
the previous season and all of a 
sudden you have the foundation 
of something special. Therefore, 
it should come as no surprise 
that Lance Taylor’s Broncos are 
expected to make a strong run 
at repeating as MAC champs this 
season. Broc Lowry threw for 
1,803 yards and rushed for 963 
more with 23 total touchdowns and 
Jalen Buckley shook off an injury-
plagued 2024 to reel off 5.6 yards 
per carry.
This is something of a throwback 
offense running double tight ends 
for OC Walt Bell, but it worked, as 
WMU played keep-away with 4.6 
yards per carry. Even though they 
only had 5.2 yards per play, a +9 
turnover margin helped and the 
defense was spectacular, shaving 
nearly two touchdowns per game 
off of their ledger year over year. 
But, it could be harder than it looks 
to stay on top.
Under 7.5
It is a luxury to be able to use 
the transfer portal for depth as 
opposed to impact, which is the 
case on offense, but the defense is 
a major work in progress. There is 
a ton of depth behind Buckley, but 
not so much behind Lowry, who 
had 203 carries, a lot for a 6-foot-1 
QB. The Broncos also don’t get the 
luxury of facing UMass and Ball 
State this season, so the conference 
slate is a bit tougher. They also 
draw Michigan, Rice, and Boise 
State in non-conference play. 
While they should beat Rice, that 
game will basically have a running 
clock with teams that averaged 49.8 
and 44.5 rush attempts per game, 
meaning the margin for error 
could be thin. My projection is 7.39 
wins.
