One of the nation’s top coaching 
jobs came from Gerad Parker, who 
captained a four-win improvement 
at Troy despite a bunch of cluster 
injuries on the offensive line and 
at the quarterback position. While 
8-6 doesn’t stand out all that much, 
especially after Jon Sumrall went 
23-5 over two seasons before 
heading to Tulane, it is a great sign 
for the future of this program. 
Many weren’t sure about the hire 
of Parker, who was the OC at 
Notre Dame, but was an attractive 
candidate to Troy because he was 
the OC at West Virginia under 
former Trojans head coach Neal 
Brown.
The last two seasons are kind of 
tough to gauge. The 4-8 start to 
Parker’s tenure came with a mass 
exodus of talent post-Sumrall, 
but the Trojans did win three of 
their last four games to end on 
something of a high note. Last 
season, the Trojans allowed 59 
sacks and put up just 2.8 yards per 
carry with their worst offensive 
season since the Larry Blakeney 
era, but found ways to win games. 
That includes a 6-3 conference 
record despite getting outgained by 
nearly a full yard per play. Usually 
that kind of good fortune regresses 
to the mean, but the never-ending 
swarm of injury bugs last season 
isn’t a guarantee to come back this 
season.
