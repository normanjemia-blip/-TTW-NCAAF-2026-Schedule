Can Jake Spavital unlock DJ Lagway? 
Lagway was once viewed as the savior in Florida. He was the 
top-ranked quarterback in the 2024 recruiting class, and he 
put some great stuff on film when he first got opportunities in 
Gainesville. Unfortunately, things turned quickly for the dual-threat 
quarterback. Last year, he threw 14 picks and took 19 sacks, making 
far too many mistakes at the most important position on the field. 
Some piled-up injuries definitely played a role in that, but it was 
clear a change in scenery was needed. Well, Lagway will now be 
playing for an elite play-caller in Spavital, and he’s taking control of 
an offense that put up 31.1 points per game last year. If the group 
up front can do a good job of keeping Lagway upright, we could see 
a major turnaround. Sawyer Robertson put up massive numbers in 
this offense, and Lagway is arguably more talented. If Lagway has 
his deep-ball touch this season, we should see plenty of big plays 
in a Spavital offense that is known for wideouts streaking down the 
field with defenders nowhere in sight. 
How will the Bears stack up in the trenches?
Keeping Lagway upright was mentioned in the previous burning 
question and it’ll be a revamped offensive line tasked with doing so. 
Aranda and Co. have Kaden Sieracki, a starter last year, back at right 
tackle, but four fresh faces were brought in at the other spots. Three 
of those four were starters. Baylor should be bigger and deeper 
along the line, so the potential is there for this group to be much 
improved from a year ago. The Bears also have a loaded defensive 
line, so there’s a chance this team is way more physical than it was 
in 2025. And overall, the squad should have the toughness required 
to compete regularly in the Big 12.  
Is the Baylor defense going to be a mess again? 
Aranda is no longer calling the defense. While he’s viewed as a great 
defensive mind, he’s wisely stepping aside and allowing a new voice 
to step in. Joe Klanderman is the guy hoping to solve Baylor’s recent 
defensive issues. Klanderman spent the last six years with Kansas 
State, and he has been open about wanting his team to be active in 
the opposing backfield. We should see a good amount of blitzing. 
That will leave the secondary a little more exposed than usual, 
but the defensive backs on this team are good enough to manage 
that. Realistically, this defense has improved at all three levels, 
so last year’s 32.6 opponent points per game should come down 
considerably.
