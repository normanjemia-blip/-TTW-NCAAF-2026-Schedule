Will Martini be shaken and stirred?
The Martini in question is Greer Martini, the 30-year-old defensive 
coordinator who has big shoes to replace with the departure of 
Chris O’Leary. O’Leary is now the DC with Jim Harbaugh and the 
Chargers, as he left the organization for a year to call plays in 
Kalamazoo and then went right back. With O’Leary, the Broncos 
were tied for fourth in the nation with 43 sacks. They were +21 
in sack differential, ranked 12th in third-down defense, and only 
allowed 24 red-zone trips in nine conference games. Martini was 
the linebackers coach and his unit was really good, but they had 
to go to the portal to replace those guys. Plus, Nadame Tucker 
and Rodney McGraw combined for 21.5 sacks and 26 QB hits, as 
Tucker had 14.5 sacks and was signed as a UDFA by O’Leary and the 
Chargers. McGraw is now with the Dolphins.
Can the offense pick up the slack?
The Broncos were just 109th in yards per play last season. Of 
course, run-pass distribution was a major factor, as they ran the ball 
over 600 times. They were seventh in total rush attempts, with three 
service academies ahead of them, plus Rice, who now runs the 
option, and Indiana. Lowry’s ability to run was the separator and 
the separator for him in taking over the job full-time after he and 
Brady Jones were in something of a timeshare to start the year. With 
Lowry and Buckley, along with a pretty experienced offensive line, 
they’ll be able to run. But, will they be able to pass enough to keep 
the opposing defense honest? 
Did Lance already have his chance?
Lance Taylor was 10-15 over his first two seasons in Kalamazoo. 
Everything seemed to come together last season, sparked by the 
pass rush and the ability to wear down the other team’s defense. 
The Broncos were 12th in average time of possession, but they were 
tied for 98th in plays of 10+ yards and tied for 110th in plays of 20+ 
yards. Under O’Leary, the defense shaved off 1.4 yards per play, 
after allowing 6.4 and 6.1 in Taylor’s first two seasons. It sure seems 
like there are a lot of reasons to be skeptical of the team given the 
heavy personnel losses on defense and an NFL-caliber defensive 
coordinator.
